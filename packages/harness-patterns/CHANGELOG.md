# @hames-ai/harness-patterns

## 0.2.0

### Minor Changes

- ae701fe: A document sanitizer seam and a bounded ZIP reader for untrusted office files (#433 S5). Both are opt-in subpaths under `stash/`; the root barrel and `./patterns` load neither.
  
  - **New `@hames-ai/harness-patterns/stash/zip.server`**: `readZip`, `writeZip`, `parseXml`, `scanXml` and `crc32`, on Node built-ins with no new dependency. The reader is for attacker-supplied archives. It refuses, with a typed `ZipRefusedError.code`, anything over 5 MiB, any archive with other than exactly one end-of-central-directory record ending the file, multi-disk, ZIP64, encryption bits 0 and 6, and any method other than stored or deflate. It also refuses more than 2,000 entries, a declared count that differs from the records parsed, overlapping entries, and local headers that disagree with the central directory. It refuses ratios over 100:1, entries over 20 MiB, totals over 50 MiB, a deflate stream that ends before the entry's compressed size, and size or CRC-32 mismatches. Names must be valid UTF-8 and at most 512 bytes, with no NUL, no absolute or drive path, no backslash and no `.`, `..` or empty segment, and must be unique after NFC and case folding. Symlink and other non-regular entries are refused. Embedded archives are never opened. Every XML part — named `.xml` or `.rels`, or whose content starts with `<` — is held to the XML limits: no DOCTYPE, no entity declarations or undeclared references (prototype keys such as `&constructor;` included), depth and attributes at most 256 each, at most 20 MiB, UTF-8 only, and namespace-aware parsing that refuses an unbound prefix. `scanXml` walks a part under the same rules without building a tree. A data-descriptor entry is accepted only when it is deflated and its descriptor equals the central directory. CRC-32 comes from a table, so `engines` stays `>=22`.
  - **New `@hames-ai/harness-patterns/stash/document-sanitizer.server`**: `flattenDocument` (tier 0) and `screenDocument` (the optional tiers 1–2).
    - `flattenDocument` verifies the declared type against the magic bytes and against the package's main part in `[Content_Types].xml`, or its ODF `mimetype`; both OOXML package parts are capped at 1 MiB and scanned without a tree. It runs an optional disarm and re-verifies its output, then converts through the sidecar with a pinned config, `DOCUMENT_CONVERT_CONFIG` (`{ output_format: 'markdown', max_archive_depth: 0, use_cache: false }`), reading at most 20 MiB of its response. It flattens every link target to its host, in time linear in the input, and runs the injection guard's deterministic sanitizer. It emits no event: the findings come back in the report, and `findingsRecordFor` turns them into a `content_sanitized` payload only for a Sanitize or Continue choice. A disarm that counted concealment it did not remove reports `hiddenContent: 'not-removed'`.
    - `screenDocument` runs an optional classifier, then an optional screen, over the stash chunks of that copy. A chunk with a tier-0 finding skips the classifier and goes to the screen. The screen only flags: a flagged chunk gets a fence and a report entry, the text is never edited, and a span that is not in the chunk is dropped.
    - `classifierFromDecide` builds the classifier from a decision function with `DOCUMENT_CLASSIFIER_POLICY`: fallback `suspicious`, calibration required, minimum confidence 0.8.
    - `sanitizeOptionFor` makes Sanitize unavailable when tier 0 throws, with a fixed reason, and never pickable unattended when hidden content was not removed.
    - Sanitize covers pdf, docx, doc, xlsx, xlsm, xls, pptx, ppt, odt, ods, odp and plain, Markdown and CSV text in v1. Every other type is refused as `unsupported-type`.
  - **`convertToMarkdown` gains two optional parameters**: a fifth, `config`, the sidecar's whole extraction config, defaulting to the previous `{ output_format: 'markdown' }`; and a sixth, `maxResponseBytes`, defaulting to no cap. **Behaviour change for ingest:** the request timeout (`DOC_CONVERT_TIMEOUT_MS`, default 120 s) now covers reading the response body as well as the headers, so a response that stalls past it is rejected.
- a462e55: Asking a human: the DX surface (#433, slice S4). Additive — the mechanism is S1–S3's, unchanged.
  
  - **`confirm(config)`** — the one-call gate at a chain boundary. Two options: Approve, which the unattended rule never picks (nothing is approved without a person, P4), and Reject — the default, the unattended choice, and by default a `stopsRun` option, so a rejection ends the run. `onReject: 'continue'` lets the chain run past the gate instead; `unattended: 'park'` waits for a person.
  - **`humanGate({ request, onAnswer })`** — the custom gate. `request(view, data)` builds any `HitlRequest`, or returns null to ask nothing; `onAnswer` runs once per DECISION — the unattended rule's pick on the run that raised it, and the replayed person's answer on the re-entry after a resume — with the shape the record holds.
  - **The runner `harness(...)` returns is bound.** `agent.resume(serialized, answers, opts?)` and `agent.continue(serialized, input, onEvent?, frame?)` carry the agent's own patterns, so a resume can only be made on the agent the pause belongs to. `resumeHarness` / `continueSession` stay for hosts that hold the pattern array themselves.
  - **`HarnessResultScoped` is a union on status [F18]**: when the status is `'paused'`, `pending` is non-optional and lists every waiting request; a `running` / `done` / `error` result has no `pending` field. The two-request consumer narrows with `if (r.status === 'paused')` and never reaches for `!`.
  - The GUIDE gains the section "Asking a human", whose typechecked snippet is the spec §6 two-request consumer; SPEC documents the gate patterns and the union.
- 6a58ab4: HITL events and the derivation (#433, slice S1). Nothing emits the new events yet: asking, pausing and the answer-bound `resumeHarness` arrive in later slices.
  
  - **Two new event types**, `hitl_request` and `hitl_response`, with their payload types `HitlRequestEventData` and `HitlResponseEventData` (both `v: 1`) and the types they use: `HitlOption`, `HitlFlag`, `HitlUnattended` and `HitlDecidedBy`. Both are always committed, so no `commitStrategy` (`on-success` after an error, `last`, `never`) drops them. `approval_request` and `approval_response` stay in `EventType`, marked `@deprecated`. **Breaking for exhaustive consumers**: `EventType` gains both new members, so a `Record<EventType, …>` or an exhaustive `switch` over it stops compiling until it handles them.
  - **`readHitl(ctx)`** (with the `HitlState` type) is the one reader. From the current run, meaning the events after the last `user_message`, it derives `pending`, the blocking requests with no response in raise order, and `answers`, the replay journal. An answer enters the journal only if it names a blocking request of the run, carries that request's kind and key, and chose an available option of it. The journal is keyed by kind, key and the set of option ids. Legacy `approval_*` events are never read, so a stored `approval_response { approved: true }` answers nothing.
  - **`answerOf(view, kind, key)`** returns the current run's answer to the `kind` request raised with `key`, the key the consumer gave it. Core composes the stored `${kind}:${key}` form itself, so a request raised with the default content-hash key has no key a consumer can name, and one that will be looked up needs an explicit `key`. It reads the view's unfiltered log, so a narrowed `viewConfig` cannot hide an answer. It returns `undefined` when two different decisions share the kind and key.
  - **Only core writes HITL events.** **Behaviour change**: `createEvent` and `trackEvent` throw for `hitl_request` and `hitl_response`, whatever `trackHistory` says. `commitEvents` and `chain()` drop any event of those two types that core did not create, and log a `console.warn` for each. No other event type is affected.
  - **Behaviour change**: `EventView.get()` always returns a new array; HITL events are deep-frozen. Before, `get()` with no filter, limit or transform returned the context's live event log, so a caller could push onto it past every guard. HITL events are frozen when core creates them (from a copy of the data it was given) and when `deserializeContext` reads them back, so a recorded decision cannot be rewritten in place.
  - **Metadata-only rendering.** `serialize()` and `serializeCompact()` render `hitl_request` as `decision requested: <kind> [<requestId>]` and `hitl_response` as `decision: <kind> → <choice | none> (<by>)`. They never render the question, summary, options, payload reference, principal, flags or resolution. **Behaviour change**: `approval_request` and `approval_response` now render as `legacy approval event` instead of a JSON dump of their payload. That includes the `approval_response` that `resumeHarness(…, approved)` still appends.
- 2ed48b7: Raise and pause (#433, slice S2). **Not released alone**: this slice can pause a run, and the answer-bound `resumeHarness` that continues one arrives in S3, so no package release happens between S2 and S4 (#433 slice plan, F19b).
  
  - **`askHuman(request)`** asks a person to decide, from inside a run: a pattern body, or a tool executor that holds no scope. It replays a decision the run already holds for the same kind, key and option set (if the choice is still an available option of the new request), applies the request's unattended rule when nobody is there, or raises a `hitl_request` and returns `pending`. Request ids are `crypto.randomUUID()`. A raised request records `resumeAt` (the top-level index and every top-level pattern name) and the run's opaque inference tier. New types: `HitlRequest`, `HitlOutcome` and `HeldResult`.
  - **Validation at raise.** An invalid request throws `HitlRequestError`: fewer than two options, duplicate option ids, a default that is missing or unavailable, an unknown `unattended` rule, `'stop'` with no `stopsRun` option, duplicate flag ids on an option, a `required` flag on an option the unattended rule may pick, and a `kind` containing `:`. `askHuman` also throws outside a run frame, in a frame with no `hitl` slot, and in one no `runChain` owns.
  - **`resolveUnattended(request)`** is the unattended rule. It never picks an option without `unattended: true`. `apply-default` takes the default when the rule may pick it, then the first available option it may pick, and otherwise stops the run with no choice. `stop` takes the first stopsRun option the rule may pick, and `park` decides nothing.
  - **`held(outcome, note?)`** builds the placeholder a gated executor returns instead of the content. **`hitlPending()`** is the stop check between steps.
  - **The run frame gains a sixth slot, `hitl: { attended }`.** On the frame it is a frozen object whose only key is `attended`, and `attended` counts only when it is exactly `true`. `amendRunFrame` passes it by reference, so it survives `withInjectionGuard` and `withSandbox`. It refuses a second one below an open one. **Behaviour change**: `harness()`, `continueSession()` and `resumeHarness()` supply `{ attended: true }` when they open the frame. They supply nothing when they join a host's frame.
  - **The per-run bookkeeping is not on the frame.** The owning context, the position, the buffer of raised events, what waits and the stop live in an async-context store of their own, held on a `Symbol.for` key so two installed copies share it. Each `runChain` that finds the slot set and no HITL run open opens one, and a nested `runChain` claims nothing. So nothing reachable from `activeRunFrame()` can write the record or suppress a pause, and two runs started concurrently in one host frame each keep their own requests.
  - **The pause.** The `runChain` that owns the HITL run commits its HITL events straight into the context after every pattern, including one that threw. A request still waiting then ends the run `paused`. The paused pattern's events are committed, but its data is not, so re-entry starts from the same data. `error` wins over a pending request. An unattended choice that stops the run ends it `done`, with a fixed response. A `runChain` nested inside one of the owner's patterns neither commits nor pauses.
  - **The stop checks.** `simpleLoop` stops after a single call and after a batch. `actorCritic` stops after a single call and after a batch, before the critic is asked. A sequential `runBatch` skips the calls after one that waits. The `chain()` combinator runs no further child. None of these records an error.
  - **Behaviour change**: `commitEvents` and `chain()` now drop EVERY `hitl_*` event a scope carries, with a `console.warn`. No legitimate HITL event travels through a scope any more, so the minted-event set S1 used is gone. This also removes the case where two installed copies of the package dropped each other's events. `hitl_request` and `hitl_response` are no longer in the always-committed set, because no commit strategy ever sees them.
  - `emitLive(event, force?)`: `force` emits whatever the pattern's `liveEvents` says. `askHuman` uses it, so the question reaches the listener while the run is still going.

This is the release-bearing changeset for human in the loop: S1 and S2 are additive, and no release happens between S2 and S4.

  **Added and changed:**
  
  - **`resumeHarness(serialized, patterns, answers, opts?)`.** `answers` maps each waiting `requestId` to an option id, or to `{ choice, flags }`, and to nothing else. `opts` is `{ principal?, resolve?, onEvent?, frame? }` (`ResumeOptions`). An answer resumes only the pause it was issued for. Every check runs before anything is recorded and before `resolve`, and a refusal throws `HitlAnswerError`, whose `code` is one of `not-paused`, `no-pending`, `expired`, `tier-changed`, `unknown-request`, `missing-answer`, `invalid-choice`, `unavailable-option`, `invalid-flag`, `required-flag` or `chain-changed`. Answers are checked against what the current run waits on, never against its journal, so an answer that was already applied is refused. A required flag must be set `true` by the answer itself.
  - **`resolve` must be idempotent per `requestId`.** If a later `resolve` throws, nothing is recorded and the blob is still paused, so a retry calls every `resolve` again.
  - **The run continues.** After the checks, `resolve` runs once per answer, inside the run frame. One `hitl_response` is recorded per answer, with `by: 'person'`, the host's `principal` and what `resolve` returned. Each held tool result becomes its outcome after `sanitizeUntrusted` (namespace `hitl`), is marked `heldBy`, and loses its `summary`; a held result is found by the UUID inside its `requestId`, so one the LLM screen fenced is still substituted. A `stopsRun` choice ends the run `done`. Otherwise the paused top-level pattern runs again, through `runChain(ctx, patterns, onEvent?, { startAt })`, and its gate replays the answer.
  - **Behaviour change: `continueSession` supersedes.** Before the new message, every request the last run still waits on gets `{ choice: null, by: 'superseded' }`, and its held results say nothing was kept.
  - **`expireHitl(serialized, now)`** closes every request past its `expiresAt` with `{ choice: null, by: 'expired' }`. That covers a blocking request of the current run and a non-blocking proposal anywhere in the log. A paused run whose request expired ends `done`, and its other pending requests are closed `{ choice: null, by: 'superseded' }`, because answers are all-or-nothing. It returns `{ serialized, expired, superseded }`, or `null` when nothing was due.
  - **Behaviour change: `compactBulkData` never summarizes a held result.**
  - **Behaviour change: the run's HITL bookkeeping store cannot write the record.** Its identity fields are non-writable, it hands out a copy of the owning context's events, and the owner commits from its buffer only what `askHuman` could have written there. Anything else is dropped with a `console.warn`.
  - `ToolResultEventData.heldBy`, and the types `HitlAnswer`, `HitlAnswers`, `HitlAnswerErrorCode` and `ResumeOptions`.
  - The observability projection previews `hitl_request` and `hitl_response` by kind and outcome, and a legacy `approval_*` event as `legacy approval event`.
- f6326c3: A single failure no longer ends a tool loop (#437 slice 1, from #425).
  
  - **`simpleLoop`** used to end on the first failed tool call, the first tool name off its allowlist, the first unparseable `tool_args`, the first multi-call turn whose calls all failed, and the first controller answer that would not parse — each with rounds left. Each is now fed back to the controller as that round's result, and the loop continues on its remaining budget. The failure costs the round, and a controller that never recovers is stopped by the budget with the usual `kind: 'budget_exhausted'` marker, or sooner by the consecutive-recovery cap (below).
  - **`actorCritic`** already fed tool failures, refusals and bad `tool_args` back through `previousAttempts`. An actor answer that would not parse now goes the same way, instead of ending the loop with attempts left.
  - **New `maxConsecutiveRecoveries` option on `SimpleLoopConfig` and `ActorCriticConfig` (default `1`)**: how many unusable answers in a row a loop feeds back. An answer is unusable when it would not parse, when its `tool_args` would not parse, when it names a tool off the allowlist, or when it is a multi-call turn of which no call could be dispatched. Up to the cap, each is fed back; the next one ends the loop exactly as the failure did before #437 — its own message, the pattern's severity and the failed call's `llmCall` — on an `error` marked with the new `ErrorEventData.kind` value `'recovery_exhausted'` and the new field `maxConsecutiveRecoveries`. A round that dispatches a tool resets the count, whatever the tool returns, and a tool that ran and failed never counts. So by default a loop stops on its second unusable answer in a row. `0` permits no recovery (`simpleLoop`'s pre-#437 behaviour), and `Infinity` leaves only the round budget. In `actorCritic`, a refusal against a tool surface that resolved to nothing (no static or dynamic names, no scoped transport, no `dynamicToolPattern`) neither counts nor resets, because the actor had no valid name to choose. **Behaviour change for `actorCritic`**: two refused tool names, two unparseable `tool_args`, or two multi-call attempts that dispatched nothing, in a row, now end the loop; before, it only ever retried them. **This binds the two sandbox agents in `@hames-ai/agents`** (`maxRetries: 6`): after two consecutive unusable answers they now stop at attempt 2, where they used to spend all 6 attempts, and the answer is composed from what they actually ran. **Breaking for exhaustive consumers**: `ErrorEventData.kind` gains `'recovery_exhausted'`.
  - **The model sees its own unparseable answer.** When an answer fails to parse for any reason other than a cut-off at the output cap or an empty completion, the feedback now quotes a bounded head of that answer (at most 400 characters), labelled as the model's previous response, next to the parser's message. Before, the next round saw only the parser's message: the turn log replays an empty action for such a round. A cut-off and an empty answer are still not quoted back.
  - **What stays fatal**: the gateway-outage refusal before a loop starts, an LLM call that never answered (transport error, timeout, abort), any failure the implementation did not classify, a `callTool` that throws, and a critic that throws. A thrown `callTool` keeps its pre-#437 behaviour everywhere: fatal when singular, and fatal in a `simpleLoop` multi-call turn whose calls all failed (`SubCallOutcome` gains `threw` so the loop can tell a throw from a returned failure). `actorCritic`'s multi-call attempts continue past a throw, as they always have.
  - **`LLMCallError` gains `recoverable`** (constructor option `{ recoverable: true }`). Only an implementation sets it, and the loops read it rather than inferring it from the message. `wrapAsLLMCallError` (`@hames-ai/harness-baml`) sets it for `BamlValidationError`, the same test as its one corrective retry. **Behaviour change for custom controllers**: an `LLMCallError` without the flag, or a plain `Error`, still ends the loop, as before.
  - **New `loop_recovery` event** (`LoopRecoveryEventData`, `LoopRecoveryFailure`): one per recovery, carrying the failure class, the verbatim error, the tool, the round and the budget, plus the failed call's `llmCall` when the model's answer is the defect. It is always committed and rendered metadata-only into LLM-facing serializations. `settleTurn`, `runChain`'s stop rule and `EventView.hasErrors()` never read it. **Breaking for exhaustive consumers**: `EventType` gains `'loop_recovery'`, so a `Record<EventType, …>` or an exhaustive `switch` over it stops compiling until it handles the new member.
  - **`actorCritic`'s in-loop `error` events become `loop_recovery`**: the refused tool name and the unparseable `tool_args` events, which the chat painted as error bubbles and the synthesizer read as a failed run after the loop had recovered (#235). A refusal against an empty allowlist is now recorded too. It was suppressed only because, as an `error`, it flooded the synthesizer's view.
  - **`compactExecution`'s thread mode** reports a failed singular call as `{ __error }`, the shape batches already use, instead of a successful `null`. Now that a loop continues past a failure, that result is the synthesizer's only record of it.
  - The cut-off feedback for `tool_args` now carries the append advice in `simpleLoop` too, from one shared builder. `actorCritic`'s unparseable-args message quotes a bounded excerpt of the args instead of the full payload, which the attempt log already replays.

- 6071a3d: An OOXML disarm for untrusted office files (#433 S6), and the two core changes it needs.

- 99387af: An approval given through `resumeHarness` now lasts only for the run it resumes (#456 c′). `resumeHarness` writes `approved` onto `ctx.data` for the resumed run. `continueSession` never cleared it, so after one approval `data.approved === true` rode every later turn, and a gate reached again went ahead without asking. `continueSession` now deletes `approved` along with `hasError`, `errorMessage` and `response`. - 3e0bdf8: Two output-cleanup fixes.
  
  - **`repairJson` / `repairJsonTracked`** (`@hames-ai/harness-patterns`) no longer fold a malformed multi-key object into its first key (#408). The last-resort handler for a single key whose bare value holds commas checked only that the input began with one key, so `{a: [x,,y], b: 1}` came back as `{ a: "[x,,y], b: 1" }`, tagged `lenient-tokens`. It now declines whenever the would-be value holds something shaped like another member (`, b:`, `"b":`, `'b':`), and the call **throws**: both loops feed it back to the model as a recovery round (#437). **Behaviour change for callers**: inputs that used to return one wrong key now throw. The decline is deliberately conservative, so some inputs the old handler got right now throw too: a Cypher label predicate or label write after a comma (`RETURN a, b:Person`, `SET a:Customer, b:Vendor`, `REMOVE …`), and a quoted word followed by a colon (Python's `if x == "y":`, once the same code also holds an ambiguous `print("a", b)`). A value that is one cleanly quoted string is exempt, so `{query: "RETURN n, n:Person"}` still repairs.
  - **`sanitizeTitle`** (`@hames-ai/agents`) takes the first line of the model's reply before it strips surrounding quotes and trailing punctuation (#409), so `"Graph Styling Tips"` followed by a second line no longer keeps its closing quote.
- 51f96c6: **`repairJson` / `repairJsonTracked`** no longer split one unquoted value into two keys at `, word:` (#453). The lenient chain's key-quoting step quoted every `, ident:` as a key. So `{query: sites like https://a.com, https://b.com}` came back as `{ query: "sites like https://a.com", https: "//b.com" }`, and `{query: MATCH (a) RETURN a, b:Person}` came back as `{ query: "MATCH (a) RETURN a", b: "Person" }`. Both results were well-formed, tagged `lenient-tokens`, and wrong. A key after a comma is now quoted only when its colon is followed by whitespace or by the start of a JSON value (`"`, `[`, `{`, a number, `true`/`false`/`null`). A colon glued to a bare word or a path is left alone. The rest of the chain then keeps the value whole, or declines it (#408) and the call throws. A glued `://` is never read as a member, so the URL list above now repairs to one `query`. **Behaviour change for callers**: inputs that used to return a split now throw. These include a Cypher label after a comma with no relationship pattern (`RETURN a, b:Person`, `SET a:Customer, b:Vendor`), which now behaves the same as the `-[r]-` form #408 already declined. They also include a compact object whose comma-led key's colon is glued to a bare word or a path (`{a:x,b:y}`, `{a: x, path:/work/in}`), and a `::` cast after a comma (`{query: SELECT id, created_at::date FROM orders}`). One input shape goes the other way: a genuine sibling whose protocol-relative or UNC value is written straight after its key's colon is now merged into the previous value, so `{q: x, src://cdn.example.com/a.js}` returns `{ q: "x, src://cdn.example.com/a.js" }` where it used to return two keys. The new rule does NOT close a `, word:` whose colon is followed by whitespace or by the start of a value: `{code: lambda a, b: a + b}` and `{query: site:example.com, intitle:"neo4j"}` both still split.
- af875e4: **`repairJson` / `repairJsonTracked`**: two quadratic regexes in the lenient chain are now linear (#461). The function as a whole is not yet linear in its input, because two bracketed-literal scanners that are not regexes are still super-linear (#463). Two regexes in the lenient chain were quadratic: the last-resort single-key handler, and the step that quotes bare values. Each had a lazy group followed by `\s*`, which re-scans a whitespace run each time the group grows by one character. The value step also re-scanned to the end of the input at every colon when no `,`, `}` or `]` followed. One malformed `tool_args` string of 200 000 characters cost ~14 s of synchronous CPU on the event loop, and model output can be steered by content the model has read. The same calls now take a few milliseconds. The handler now slices its value between the colon and the final `}`. The value step takes its value greedily and trims it afterwards, and it runs only up to the last terminator. Results are unchanged: a differential fuzz of 300 000 inputs against the previous implementation found no difference in any return value or thrown error.
- 94870a1: **`repairJson` / `repairJsonTracked`**: the bracketed-literal scanner in the lenient chain is now linear in its input, and the chain refuses input over 16 384 characters (#463). The scanner had three super-linear shapes. An unbalanced `[` or `{` after a colon was scanned to the end of the input at every colon, which is quadratic: ~23 s of CPU at 200 000 characters. A nested literal was re-scanned at every level of a recursive repair, so it cost its depth times its length until the recursion ran out of stack. And nested objects whose innermost value cannot be repaired paid that cost at every nested colon, which is cubic: ~68 s at 16 000 characters and ~10 minutes at 200 000. Each shape now costs about a millisecond at 16 000 characters. One pass decides, bottom-up, whether every literal can be repaired, and a second pass renders only the literals that are parked. Neither recurses. Results are unchanged, with one exception: a differential fuzz of 1.6 million inputs against the previous implementation found no difference in any return value or thrown error, except on literals nested deeper than the old recursion's stack (past ~2 000 levels, depending on JIT state). Those threw `RangeError` and now repair. **Behaviour change for callers**: input longer than 16 384 characters (after trimming) that is neither valid JSON nor recoverable by the unescaped-content strategy now throws a `SyntaxError` that names the length and the limit, even where the lenient chain would have repaired it. It is never truncated. Both loops forward the refusal to the model, so it is told to send strict JSON, not only that its args did not parse. Valid JSON of any length, and the unescaped-content recovery of long payloads, run before the bound and are unaffected. The bound is defence in depth: with the scanner fixed, no step of the function is known to be super-linear. One limit that is not about cost remains: the unescaped-content strategy still recurses, so input it reads several thousand levels deep throws `RangeError`, in about a millisecond, as it did before.
